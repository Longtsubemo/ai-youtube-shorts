# Finance safety - important

This channel is educational. General discussion of financial concepts is
expected and encouraged. Personalized directives are not.

- Do NOT write "you should buy", "you must invest in", "this stock will", or
  any variant that tells an individual what to do with their own money.
- Do NOT predict prices, returns, or outcomes.
- Do NOT frame anything as personalized investment, tax, or financial advice.
- Instead, explain the concept or mechanism, and let the viewer draw their own
  conclusion. Describe what tends to happen and why.

# Writing for the ear

- Write for spoken narration, not as an essay. Short sentences. No bullet
  points, no headings, no stage directions inside the narration.
- It must sound natural when read aloud: no jargon without an immediate
  plain-language gloss.
- Open strongly and immediately - curiosity in the first sentence.
- Build toward a payoff or reveal rather than listing facts.
- Use a concrete example where one helps.
- Assume a broad audience with no finance background.

# Length budget

Length is decided by **how many words are actually spoken**, not by how long
you imagine the video feels. Use words as your unit and derive seconds from
them.

- Total spoken narration: **165-190 words**, counting only the words inside
  `narration`. That is the whole script.
- Natural delivery for a finance Short runs about **2.5 spoken words per
  second** (roughly 150 words per minute). At that pace 165-190 words is
  around 66-76 seconds, which sits inside the {target_duration_seconds}-second
  target and clearly above the {duration_min_seconds}-second floor.
- Count the words once, across the whole script. The `sections` **split** that
  same narration; they do not add words to it and nothing may be repeated
  between `narration` and the sections.
- Do not pad the script with filler to reach a number, and do not cram the idea
  into a breathless run. If the material is thin, stay inside the band by
  keeping sentences short and concrete rather than by padding.
- Stay natural. The budget is a guide for length, not a rhythm to recite. Do
  not mention counting, timing, or this budget in the narration itself.

Per section, take your share of the total and compute timing from the words you
actually wrote:

- aim for roughly `total words / number of sections` words per section, letting
  the hook and the payoff run a little longer than the middle
- `estimated_seconds` = that section's word count / 2.5, rounded to a whole
  second
- the `estimated_seconds` values should add up to a total between 65 and 80

# What NOT to produce

- No web research, no browsing, no searches.
- No citations, no URLs, no source names, no links.
- No claims about verifiability, corroboration, or whether evidence exists.
- No quality score, no PASS/FAIL, no confidence rating, no verdict.
- No lines for the mascot.

# Fields to fill

- `title`: the on-screen title for the Short.
- `narration`: the full spoken script, 165-190 words. The sections together
  carry exactly these same words, so do not repeat or expand anything here.
- `sections`: 3-6 ordered segments. Together their `narration` fields must
  contain exactly the same words as `narration` - the same script, split up.
  - `section_id`: sequential id such as "s1", "s2".
  - `role`: what the beat does - for example hook, setup, explanation, example,
    reveal, payoff, closing.
  - `narration`: the exact words spoken in this segment. Never empty. All
    sections together must total 165-190 words.
  - `estimated_seconds`: this section's word count divided by 2.5, rounded to a
    whole second. Compute it from the words you actually wrote - do not guess it
    and do not aim it at the target.
  - `visual_hint`: one short line describing what the fox mascot or the scene
    could be doing on screen. This is a visual note, never spoken words.
