# Content Strategy

Design reference for what the channel produces and why, so topic
selection, research, and script generation stay aligned with a single,
consistent channel identity.

## 1. Channel positioning

Faceless explainer channel for **people who enjoy learning interesting
things quickly**.

- Format: YouTube Shorts, vertical 9:16, **30–60 seconds**.
- Tone: clear, concise, curious, substantive.
- No presenter identity; visuals + voiceover carry the content.

## 2. Content pillars

| Pillar | Examples |
|---|---|
| Artificial intelligence | How models work, practical AI phenomena |
| Technology | Computing, engineering, hardware, software curiosities |
| Science | Biology, physics, chemistry, psychology, methods |
| Space | Astronomy, missions, cosmic phenomena |
| Interesting/curious phenomena | Unusual facts, surprising cause-and-effect, historical oddities |

Topics are **not restricted to recent discoveries**. A topic can be:

- evergreen
- trending
- recently discovered
- historical
- surprising facts
- technological developments
- scientific concepts
- unusual phenomena

Freshness is one signal among many, relevant only when the subject is
genuinely time-sensitive (e.g. a new discovery or mission milestone).
Evergreen and historical topics have lasting value and are deliberately
viable.

## 3. Fixed story format

Every video follows the same narrative shape regardless of subject:

1. **Hook** — within the first ~2 seconds, a curiosity gap or surprise
   that justifies watching.
2. **Simple explanation/story** — one idea, explained intuitively.
3. **Visual progression** — images/clips that track the explanation so
   the viewer is always looking at what is being said.
4. **Interesting payoff** — a satisfying resolution, bigger picture, or
   "wait, so that's why" moment.
5. **Concise ending** — one final line, no drawn-out outro.

The format is fixed; the subject varies. Repetition of *format* is fine;
repetition of *content* is not (see `POLICY_AND_SAFETY.md`).

## 4. What makes a good Short here

For the brief length, a topic must be explainable in a single idea:

- **Explainable:** one core idea that fits a 30–60 s mental model.
- **Visually presentable:** the idea can be shown, not just narrated.
- **Curiosity strong:** hook potential (a gap between what viewers think
  and what is true).
- **Substantive:** grounded in verifiable evidence, not vibes.

Topic-format fit (explainer potential, visual potential, curiosity
potential) is scored in topic selection; see `TOPIC_SELECTION.md`.

## 5. Research and writing standards derived from strategy

- Claims must be evidence-backed and traceable (`RESEARCH_PIPELINE.md`,
  `FACT_VERIFICATION.md`).
- The explanation must distinguish facts, inferences, and speculations
  — a 45-second video should not present a hypothesis as established
  fact.
- Script writing targets spoken clarity: short sentences, active voice,
  one idea per sentence, natural rhythm for 30–60 s.

## 6. Anti-strategy (what we avoid)

- Content farms / mass-produced near-duplicate variations of the same
  video.
- Misleading framing or exaggerated claims purely for clicks.
- Clickbait that the payoff does not deliver.
- Subject matter that cannot be explained substantively in the format.

These constraints are not just editorial taste; they are required for
YouTube policy safety (`POLICY_AND_SAFETY.md`).
